## Primary sources reviewed

1. EPA, Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Program: https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-renovation-repair-and-painting-program
   - EPA states the RRP Rule generally applies to paid work disturbing painted surfaces in pre-1978 homes, child-care facilities, and preschools.
   - Firms must be certified and workers trained in lead-safe work practices; emergency disaster provision exists for certain requirements.
   - EPA administers the program in most jurisdictions and lists authorized state/tribal programs.
   - EPA distinguishes RRP from lead abatement.

2. OSHA, 29 CFR Part 1926 construction standards: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926
   - The construction standards index includes provisions for training, injury reporting, PPE, respiratory protection, fire prevention, hazardous communication, lead, HAZWOPER, electrical, fall protection, silica, and asbestos.
   - Individual provisions must be checked for scope, exceptions, and current text before operational decisions.

3. EPA, Asbestos Laws and Regulations: https://www.epa.gov/asbestos/asbestos-laws-and-regulations
   - EPA identifies AHERA, TSCA, CAA, SDWA, CERCLA, NESHAP, worker-protection rules, and related federal sources.
   - EPA explains that asbestos NESHAP work practices and notification can apply to covered demolitions/renovations, subject to scope and thresholds; local/state requirements may also apply.
   - The source warns against treating all asbestos rules as one universal requirement.

4. NAIC, Model Laws: https://content.naic.org/model-laws
   - Insurance regulation is state-based. NAIC model laws are not automatically law in every state; state adoption pages identify enacted or similar legislation.
   - The NAIC site links to a state insurance department directory: https://content.naic.org/state-insurance-departments

## Research boundaries

- No proprietary Xactimate pricing databases, carrier manuals, or copyrighted standards are reproduced.
- State/local licensing, permitting, claims-handling, and code requirements are not generalized. Any record without verified state/local authority is marked UNKNOWN or ATLAS_HEURISTIC and is not presented as law.
- The deliverable is an initial, source-backed corpus scaffold/seed release with authoritative federal records, ontology/workflow records, all 50 states + DC profile placeholders, and explicit gaps. It is not a substitute for legal advice or a completed legal survey of every locality.
- A refresh cadence and validation rules are included so a later research run can expand state and local coverage.

## Key classification decisions

- Federal regulatory requirements: LAW/REGULATION/SAFETY_REQUIREMENT/ENVIRONMENTAL_REQUIREMENT with Tier 1 provenance.
- NAIC model law material: PROFESSIONAL/INDUSTRY reference metadata; not state law unless the actual state enactment is verified.
- Workflow guidance: CONTRACTOR_WORKFLOW, DOCUMENTATION_REQUIREMENT, EVIDENCE_REQUIREMENT, INDUSTRY_PRACTICE, BEST_PRACTICE, or ATLAS_HEURISTIC as appropriate.
- Potential supplement/revenue items are never marked automatically payable; they are subject to policy, facts, jurisdiction, documentation, and carrier determination.
